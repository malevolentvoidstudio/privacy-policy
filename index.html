Privacy Policy

Battery Charge Alarm and Heat Detector

Last updated: September 26, 2026

This policy explains what data the Battery Charge Alarm and Heat Detector app ("the app") collects, why, and how it's handled. The app is built to run almost entirely on your device — most of what it does never leaves your phone.

What stays on your device

The following information is read and stored locally on your phone only. It is never sent to us or to any server we control:

  • Battery charge level, temperature, and charging status, read from your device's battery sensors while the app is monitoring.
  • Your chosen alarm target percentage, custom ringtone selections, and theft alarm settings.
  • Charging history — a local log of past charge sessions, stored only on your device.
  • Battery health, capacity, and type information, read directly from your device.

None of the above is transmitted anywhere. If you uninstall the app, this data is deleted along with it.

What we do collect

Feedback you choose to submit

If you use the in-app feedback form, the message you write is sent to our contact email through a third-party form-relay service (Web3Forms). We only receive what you type — nothing else about your device is attached to it.

Advertising data (via Google AdMob)

The app shows ads using Google AdMob. AdMob may collect an advertising identifier and other data to serve and measure ads, in line with Google's own privacy practices. We do not receive or store this data ourselves — it is handled directly between your device and Google. See Google's policy below for details on what AdMob collects.

Camera use for the Theft Alarm feature (optional)

What it does

If you turn on the Theft Alarm and separately choose to enable photo capture, the app will use your device's front-facing camera to take a short series of up to three photos — spaced a few seconds apart — at the moment a theft alert is triggered (i.e. when the charger is unplugged unexpectedly before your target battery level is reached). This happens automatically, without opening a camera preview, so you will not see a viewfinder when it happens.

Why it's handled this way

The purpose of this feature is to help identify who may have disconnected your charging device without permission. A visible camera preview would defeat that purpose, so the capture happens in the background while the alarm itself is sounding on screen.

Where the photos go

Photos are saved only to private storage inside the app on your own device — the same private storage area other apps cannot access. They are never uploaded to us, to any server, or to any third party. You can view them at any time from the app's Security Photos screen, and delete individual photos or clear all of them whenever you choose. The app also automatically removes the oldest photos once a reasonable number have built up, so storage doesn't grow indefinitely.

Your control over this

This feature is off by default. It only activates if you (1) turn on Theft Alarm and (2) separately tap "Enable Photo Capture" and grant camera permission when asked. You can revoke camera permission at any time in your device's app settings, which disables this feature entirely.
